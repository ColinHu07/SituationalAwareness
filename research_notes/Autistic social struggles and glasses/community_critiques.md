# Autistic Community and Neurodiversity-Scholar Critiques of Social-Assistance Tech (and What They Endorse)

Legend: **[SCHOLARLY]** = peer-reviewed study or scholarly analysis. **[COMMUNITY]** = autistic community opinion, advocacy, or social-media analysis. **[PRESS]** = journalism. Items marked "(snippet only)" come from search-result summaries, not a full-text read.

## 1. Double empathy problem and its evidence: what it means for tech that "fixes" autistic people

### Takeaway
Autistic-to-autistic communication works about as well as non-autistic-to-non-autistic communication. Rapport drops in mixed pairs, and non-autistic people judge autistic people negatively within seconds, based on style rather than content. So a tool that only coaches the autistic person puts all the work on one side of a two-sided problem.

### Cited Findings
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Milton (2012) coined the "double empathy problem." It recasts autistic social difficulty as a two-way breakdown of reciprocity and mutual understanding, not a one-sided "theory of mind" deficit. Published in *Disability & Society* 27(6):883–887. — [Milton 2012 (Kent repository PDF)](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/62639/1/Double%20empathy%20problem.pdf); [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ980919)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Crompton et al. (2020), diffusion-chain study: 72 participants in 9 chains of 8 people each (3 all-autistic, 3 all-non-autistic, 3 mixed) passed along a 30-point story. All-autistic chains did **not** differ significantly from all-non-autistic chains. Mixed chains lost detail significantly faster and had significantly lower rapport ratings. — [Crompton et al. 2020, *Autism*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1362361320919286); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32431157)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Crompton et al. (2025), larger replication in *Nature Human Behaviour*: 311 participants in Scotland, England and the USA. It found **no significant difference** in information transfer across autistic, non-autistic and **mixed** chains. Rapport was higher with people of the same neurotype and when diagnosis was disclosed. Crompton: autistic and non-autistic people communicate differently, but "it is just as successful." She also said the field has focused on "fixing" autistic communication. — [ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/05/250514111351.htm); [Nature Human Behaviour article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02163-z)
  - **Note (a conflict with 2020):** the 2020 study found worse *information transfer* in mixed chains. The 2025 replication, about 4x larger, did not; only the *rapport* gap held up. The accurate summary: the mismatch shows up most reliably in rapport and comfort, and less reliably in raw information transfer.
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Foster, Crompton, Sasson et al. (2025) studied rapport in same-neurotype and mixed-neurotype groups of autistic and non-autistic adults (*Autism*). (Title/snippet only; I did not read the detailed results.) — [Foster et al. 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13623613251320444)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Sasson et al. (2017), three studies: non-autistic observers formed far less favorable first impressions of autistic people from "thin slices" (a few seconds) of real behavior. They were also less willing to interact with them. The bias appeared within seconds, did not fade with more exposure, and held for children and adults. It **disappeared when observers saw only conversational content without audio-visual cues**, meaning style, not substance, drives the negative impressions. — [Sasson et al. 2017, *Scientific Reports*](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep40700)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Research on emotion expression suggests the problem runs both ways: non-autistic people also have trouble reading autistic facial expressions. (Snippet-level; see the linked review for detail.) — [Can Neurotypical Individuals Read Autistic Facial Expressions? (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4975602/); [Scientific American on double empathy](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/autism-human-connection-and-the-double-empathy-problem/)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Some newer work adds nuance. Studies using the "empathic accuracy" method (Cheang et al. 2025, *Autism*) test double empathy more finely, and results are not uniformly supportive. (Title-level only; I did not read the results.) — [Cheang et al. 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13623613241252320); [Nuances of double empathy (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13501596/)

### Inferences
- A glasses app framed as "helping autistic people read neurotypicals" treats half of a mutual mismatch as the whole problem. Evidence *suggests* (it does not prove) that the bigger, sturdier gap is rapport and first-impression bias by non-autistic people, which coaching the autistic person cannot fix.
- Sasson's result (bias disappears when only content is visible) suggests that coaching autistic people to change their *style* (eye contact, facial expression, prosody) serves non-autistic observers' bias rather than the quality of communication.
- Design stance to take from this: position the app as a **translator between two communication styles** (both directions), not a corrector of the wearer. Disclosure seems to help rapport (Crompton 2025), so disclosure support may be a legitimate feature, but only as the user's choice (see section 6).

### Gaps
- I could not open the full text of Crompton 2025 (paywall/redirect), so I have no effect sizes or exact rapport numbers.
- No study I found tests whether a real-time AI mediator narrows or widens the double-empathy gap.

## 2. Critiques of "normalizing" tech, deficit framing in HCI, and emotion-recognition AI

### Takeaway
Autistic and critical HCI scholars have documented, with numbers, that most autism tech research aims to make autistic people look more neurotypical. They argue this harms users, removes their agency, and turns autism into a showcase use case for emotion AI and surveillance. Facial emotion recognition for autism in particular has weak evidence and draws strong criticism.

### Cited Findings
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Williams & Gilbert (2020), "Perseverations of the Academy" (*International Journal of Human-Computer Studies*), led by autistic scholar Rua M. Williams. Of the wearable autism interventions reviewed, **90% were "normalizing technologies"** that treat autistic traits as deficits to overwrite with neurotypical behavior. **50% focused on social-skills training or emotional-intelligence assessment.** They argue the field's fixation on autism as a social-skills deficit, rather than a marginalized identity, may cause direct harm. — [Williams & Gilbert 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581920300872) (figures via search snippet)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Spiel, Frauenberger, Keyes & Fitzpatrick (2019), "Agency of Autistic Children in Technology Research" (*ACM TOCHI*), a critical literature review. The technologies embody the normative expectations of a neurotypical society that mostly sees autism as a medical deficit needing "correction." Autistic children, the supposed beneficiaries, become a **secondary audience** to purposes defined by others (parents, therapists, teachers). Few designs serve autistic people's own interests and desires. — [Spiel et al. 2019, ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3344919); [Author PDF](https://ironholds.org/resources/papers/autistic_agency.pdf)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Keyes (2020), "Automating autism" (*Journal of Sociotechnical Critique*). AI research narratives about "identifying" or "solving" autism reproduce the denial of autistic personhood. Fairness-focused AI ethics cannot address this, because the harm is in how autistic people are *described*, not in how benefits are shared out. — [Keyes 2020](https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/sociotechnicalcritique/vol1/iss1/8/); [PDF](https://ironholds.org/resources/papers/automating_autism.pdf)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Nagy (2024), "Autism and the making of emotion AI" (*New Media & Society* 26(7)). Facial emotion-recognition developers repeatedly used autism as a "charismatic use-case," a source of concepts, and a testbed. This helped turn emotional data into a corporate asset. Disability became a resource for **surveillance capitalism**. — [Nagy 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/14614448221109550)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Katirai (2025, *Autism*), "Autism and emotion recognition technologies in the workplace," critically examines emotion recognition technology aimed at autistic workers. (I could not read the full text: HTTP 403.) — [Katirai 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613241279704)
- **[SCHOLARLY/PRESS]** On Stanford's "Superpower Glass" (Google Glass emotion cues for autistic children), *Spectrum/The Transmitter* reported expert skepticism. Results were weak, perhaps because the tool "targets the wrong problem": autistic children may not have trouble recognizing emotions. There was no significant difference on some social-skills and emotion-recognition tests, and gains on the Vineland scale had **disappeared six weeks after treatment ended**. — [The Transmitter](https://www.thetransmitter.org/spectrum/tech-firms-superpower-glass-autism-not-super-experts-say/) (snippet only)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** One study found that emotion-recognition difficulty in autistic adult women **correlated with alexithymia, not autism**. This undercuts the premise that autistic people as a group need emotion labels. — [PMC7539601](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7539601/) (title-level)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Begel et al. (2020, ASSETS), Microsoft Research video-calling prototype for autistic adults (an "Expressiveness Mirror" showing emoji for Ekman's 6 emotions plus an expressiveness bar). Lessons included:
  - The AI was trained by **(presumed) neurotypical raters on (presumed) neurotypical faces**, and the authors acknowledged the mismatch of applying that data to autistic users.
  - Participants doubted its accuracy (P12: "For the most part no. Happy was lighting up all…").
  - Participants wanted recognition of **much more complex cognitive and emotional states** than 6 basic emotions.
  - Participants did not know how to act on meter readings (P11: the partner's bar "was much longer, but, I did not know…").
  - Some participants were enthusiastic (P2: "1000% I want this"). Participants also asked for a version that interprets the **partner's** face.
  - — [Begel et al. 2020 PDF](https://andrewbegel.com/papers/lessons-learned-autism.pdf); [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3373625.3418305)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Sum, Alharbi, Spektor, Bennett, Harrington, Spiel & Williams (CHI 2022 workshop), "Dreaming Disability Justice in HCI." It frames disability justice as centering disabled people's needs and expertise. It critiques single-identity focus and asks how ableism overlaps with racism, classism, sexism and colonialism. — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3491101.3503731); [Workshop site](https://disabilityjusticeinhci.org/)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Jang, Carrington & Begel (CHI 2026), "From Autonomy to Sovereignty – A New Telos for Socially Assistive Technology." Assistive technology research over-values *independence*, while disabled people value *interdependence*. The goal should be "relational sovereignty": the question shifts from "Can they do it?" to "Do they get to decide?" It proposes justice-oriented reflection questions and "sovereign technical primitives." — [arXiv 2603.07737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07737)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** A 2024 metareview, "Love, Joy, and Autism Robots," critiques deficit framing in autism robotics. (Found in search; not read.) — [arXiv 2403.05098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.05098)

### Inferences
- A smart-glasses app that shows emotion labels on faces sits squarely in the category these scholars criticize most: normalizing, deficit-framed, emotion-AI. It also carries the scientific problems of basic-emotion classifiers, which were trained on neurotypical data and show poor accuracy on complex states.
- Begel's participants still wanted *something*. The demand is real, but it points to richer, contextual interpretation offered on request, not constant labels.

### Gaps
- I did not locate the specific Rizvi et al. paper named in the brief. The closest match is Jang et al. (CHI 2024), in section 4.
- I found no formal ASAN position statement specifically on emotion-recognition wearables.

## 3. Masking/camouflaging: do coaching tools add to the burden? What autistic people say about social scripts

### Takeaway
Masking, including preparing social scripts and forcing eye contact, is linked to exhaustion, burnout, and worse mental health. A real-time coach that feeds the user neurotypical "correct" responses risks automating and deepening masking, a risk now documented in how autistic people use ChatGPT.

### Cited Findings
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Pearson & Rose (2021, *Autism in Adulthood*; Kieran Rose is autistic): masking means conscious and unconscious strategies to suppress autistic traits and "pass." Examples include **preparing scripts for social interactions**, restraining stimming, and keeping up eye contact. It is driven by stigma and creates an "illusion of choice." The constant self-monitoring is effortful and exhausting. It may reduce external harms (bullying) while increasing internal ones (exhaustion, burnout). — [Pearson & Rose 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/aut.2020.0043); [Accepted manuscript](https://community.opogo.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Pearson-RoseConceptualAnalysisAuthorAccepted.pdf)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Masking is linked to late or missed diagnosis, mental health problems, burnout and suicidality. — [Pearson & Rose 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/aut.2020.0043)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** A 2025 systematic review covers the consequences of social camouflaging in autistic adults. Mantzalas et al. (2024) measured and validated autistic burnout. (Titles/snippets only.) — [Consequences of camouflaging review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3050656525000288); [Mantzalas 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/aur.3129)
- **[SCHOLARLY, built on COMMUNITY data]** "I Use ChatGPT to Humanize My Words" (arXiv 2601.17946, 2026) analyzed **3,984 social-media posts** (e.g., Reddit) by self-identified autistic users.
  - Benefits: managing executive dysfunction, emotion regulation, *translating neurotypical communication*, and affirming autistic identity.
  - Risks: **"identity erasure through masking"** (using AI to conform to neurotypical norms), delusional reinforcement, and conflicts with users' sense of justice.
  - Recommendations: "beneficial friction," "**bidirectional translation**," and keeping emotional validation separate from reality.
  - — [arXiv 2601.17946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17946)
- **[COMMUNITY]** Autistic advocate Kieran Rose's writing on masking. — [The Autistic Advocate](https://theautisticadvocate.com/autistic-masking/)

### Inferences
- Live coaching through glasses ("smile now," "make eye contact," "say X") is literally an external script feed. It likely adds to the masking load: the user must monitor the conversation *and* the display *and* perform. This is an inference; no study directly measures masking with a wearable coach.
- Scripts are not bad in themselves. Many autistic people use scripts by choice. The harm shows up when scripts are imposed to hide autistic traits. Design implication: user-written, user-invoked scripts are fine; system-pushed "correct behavior" prompts are risky.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical study measuring camouflaging-scale or burnout changes from using a real-time social-coaching wearable.

## 4. What autistic people say they WANT from tech (participatory design findings)

### Takeaway
When autistic adults set the agenda, they ask for control, reversibility, protected attention, sensory respect, private help interpreting ambiguous communication (including explanations of neurotypical behavior), two-way translation, and help with energy and stress. They do not ask to be made to look normal.

### Cited Findings
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Interviews with 106 autistic adults in Chile (*Disability and Rehabilitation: Assistive Technology*, 2025/26):
  - Technology supported self-determination when it **preserved authorship of decisions, provided simple refusal and reversible settings, protected attention, and respected privacy and sensory needs**.
  - Agency was undermined when **configurations were imposed by others, automation was opaque, or notifications and sensory stimuli were excessive**.
  - Other themes: digital scaffolds for planning and pacing, feedback calibrated to *personal* goals, and "consented interdependence."
  - — [PubMed 41231666](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41231666/); [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17483107.2025.2586641)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Jang et al. (CHI 2024), "It's the only thing I can trust": 11 autistic workers compared a GPT-4 advisor with a disguised human. **9 of 11 preferred the LLM**, and 7 would consider it a primary go-to resource for workplace communication. A job-coach specialist found some LLM advice unhelpful or unsuitable for autistic people, for example "just walk up to people and start talking." — [CMU News](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2024/june/people-with-autism-turn-to-chatgpt-for-advice-on-workplace-issues); [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642894); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03297)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Autistic users already use ChatGPT to translate neurotypical communication and to regulate emotions. Researchers recommend **bidirectional translation**. — [arXiv 2601.17946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17946)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Zolyomi et al. (CSCW 2023), "Designing for Common Ground." Autistic adults and a *trusted conversation partner* mapped their conversation patterns together, and autistic participants designed a game to support **shared** common ground in mixed conversations. The support is aimed at the pair, not the autistic person alone. — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3610057)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Speculative design with neurodiverse pairs on an "Emotion Translator" (title-level only). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380520135_An_Emotion_Translator_Speculative_Design_By_Neurodiverse_Dyads)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Begel et al. participants asked for help interpreting the **partner's** expressions (an "Expressiveness Prosthetic"), not just feedback on their own face. — [Begel et al. 2020](https://andrewbegel.com/papers/lessons-learned-autism.pdf)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** A participatory design study with 20 autistic adults examined coping with social-media stress. (Abstract-level only.) — [ACM 3757593](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3757593)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** A systematic review of participatory methods with autistic people found six adaptation themes: suitable methods, individual differences, flexibility, communication, **environment and sensory issues**, and challenging assumptions. Sensory needs conflict between people: some avoid stimuli, others seek them. — [PMC11300472](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11300472/)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** CHI 2025 scoping review of autistic adults' involvement in participatory tech design. (Title-level.) — [ACM 3706598.3713961](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713961)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Relational sovereignty: users should decide when to act independently and when to rely on others. — [Jang, Carrington & Begel 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07737)

### Inferences
- Wanted features map onto the brief's list as follows:
  - **Private decoding of ambiguous language:** supported by the ChatGPT-translation and Begel partner-interpretation findings.
  - **Explaining the neurotypical side:** the same evidence supports it.
  - **Energy and stress pacing:** supported by the planning/pacing scaffolds in the Chile study.
  - **Sensory management:** supported by the Chile study and the participatory review.
  - **Two-way translation:** supported by the arXiv 2026 paper and Zolyomi.
- Energy/"spoon" tracking and exit strategies are plausible fits, but I found **no direct study** of them.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study specifically on "spoon"/energy tracking or "exit strategy" features for autistic adults.

## 5. Sensory overload and cognitive load of heads-up / real-time cues

### Takeaway
Autistic adults already report sensory overstimulation and cognitive overload in mediated conversations. Excessive notifications and sensory stimuli are named as things that erode agency. Real-time cue streams during conversation risk adding load. In the one autism study of live AI feedback, participants reported mild distraction and trouble knowing how to act on the cues.

### Cited Findings
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Zolyomi et al. (CSCW 2019), "Managing Stress: The Needs of Autistic Adults in Video Calling." Stress in video calls is caused by **sensory overstimulation, cognitive overload, and anxiety** (as summarized in Begel et al. 2020). — [ACM 3359236](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3359236); [Begel et al. 2020](https://andrewbegel.com/papers/lessons-learned-autism.pdf)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** In Begel et al., participants rated distraction. Comments included cues being "accurate and a little distracting." The authors noted the **"cognitive overhead required to interpret it in real-time"**. Emoji were preferred as the least demanding format. — [Begel et al. 2020](https://andrewbegel.com/papers/lessons-learned-autism.pdf)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Excessive notifications and sensory stimuli undermine autistic adults' agency with technology. — [PubMed 41231666](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41231666/)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Stanford's Superpower Glass team published "Delivering Unobtrusive Real-time Social Cues in Wearable Systems," which shows that unobtrusiveness was treated as a key design problem. — [arXiv 2002.06581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06581)

### Inferences
- A heads-up display adds a visual stream at the very moment attention is most taxed. Defaults should be **silent/pull-based** (the user asks for help), with glanceable, minimal output. Offering after-the-fact review, which carries no live load, is likely safer than live cues. This is an inference from the evidence above.

### Gaps
- I found no autism-specific study measuring cognitive load (e.g., NASA-TLX) for optical heads-up display social cues in face-to-face conversation. The SocialMind (arXiv 2412.04036) PDF could not be parsed, so I could not check whether it included autistic users.

## 6. Privacy and consent for bystanders, disability disclosure, and surveillance

### Takeaway
Camera glasses that analyze other people's faces create real risk to bystanders (identity lookup has already been demonstrated). Emotion AI is historically tied to surveillance. Autistic adults say configurations imposed by others (parents, employers) and opaque automation undermine their agency.

### Cited Findings
- **[PRESS]** In 2024, Harvard students built "I-XRAY" on Ray-Ban Meta glasses plus PimEyes face search. It went from a face to a name, phone number and home address in real time. They did not release the code. It shows how ordinary-looking glasses can enable doxxing and stalking. — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/someone-put-facial-recognition-tech-onto-metas-smart-glasses-to-instantly-dox-strangers/); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnkoetsier/2024/10/03/metas-ray-ban-smart-glasses-used-to-instantly-dox-strangers-in-public-thanks-to-ai-and-facial-recognition/); [Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2024/10/19/ai-glasses/)
- **[PRESS, lower reliability]** A 2026 blog reports AI-glasses "bans and backlash." I have not verified it against primary sources. — [SolidAITech (Aug 2026)](https://www.solidaitech.com/2026/08/ai-glasses-bans-backlash-meta.html)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Emotion AI grew partly by using autism as a testbed. Emotional data became a corporate asset for surveillance capitalism. — [Nagy 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/14614448221109550)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Katirai (2025) examines emotion recognition technology aimed at autistic people **in the workplace**, a direct employer-monitoring concern. — [Katirai 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613241279704)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Autism tech often serves parents, therapists and teachers as the primary audience, not autistic users. — [Spiel et al. 2019](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3344919)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Stanford's Autism Glass **recorded the amount and type of eye contact**, which is monitoring data about the wearer. — [Autism Glass Project](https://autismglass.stanford.edu/); [PMC7203617 (gaze phenotyping)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7203617)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Configurations imposed by others and opaque automation undermine agency; respecting privacy supports it. — [PubMed 41231666](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41231666/)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Disclosing a diagnosis was associated with higher rapport in the 2025 diffusion-chain study. This matters because a visible device may disclose disability involuntarily. — [ScienceDaily on Crompton 2025](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/05/250514111351.htm)

### Inferences
- Visible AI glasses may **out** the wearer as disabled, or be read as recording. Either way the user should control when and how disclosure happens. Disclosure *can* help rapport, but only when chosen.
- Any caregiver or employer dashboard reproduces the "secondary audience" harm. Wearer-only data is the safer default.

### Gaps
- I found no autism-specific study on how bystanders react to someone wearing assistive AI glasses, or on the wearer's disclosure anxiety with such glasses.

## 7. Autistic reactions specifically to AI smart-glasses social coaches (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
I found **no reliable, citable collection** of autistic community reactions (Reddit, Bluesky, autistic journalists) specifically about 2024–2026 AI smart-glasses social coaches. The closest evidence is older critique of emotion-recognition Glass projects, plus 2026 analysis of autistic ChatGPT use.

### Cited Findings
- **[COMMUNITY/SCHOLARLY]** 3,984 autistic social-media posts about ChatGPT show both benefits (translation, regulation) and masking/identity-erasure risks. This is the closest large-scale proxy for community sentiment about AI social mediators. — [arXiv 2601.17946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17946)
- **[PRESS]** Earlier Glass projects (Superpower Glass, Brain Power) were covered mostly uncritically in mainstream press, with expert skepticism reported in *Spectrum*. — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/upgraded-google-glass-helps-autistic-kids-see-emotions); [The Transmitter](https://www.thetransmitter.org/spectrum/tech-firms-superpower-glass-autism-not-super-experts-say/)
- **[SCHOLARLY]** Cambridge's Autism Research Centre runs an active "Smart Glasses" project on real-time conversational feedback about social cues. — [ARC Smart Glasses](https://www.autismresearchcentre.com/projects/smart-glasses/)
- **[PRESS/hackathon]** Hobby projects exist (e.g., "ClearCue," smart-glasses social cues for autistic users). — [Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/clear-cue/updates/726519)

### Inferences
- Community reaction to glasses coaches will probably follow the established critiques: normalizing, masking burden, and surveillance. This is an inference from the documented reactions to emotion AI and ChatGPT masking, not observed data about glasses.

## Synthesis: Do / Don't design principles (each tied to sources above)

| # | DO | DON'T | Basis |
|---|----|-------|-------|
| 1 | Frame the app as a **two-way translator** between communication styles. Explain the neurotypical side ("people often mean X by this") and help the user explain themselves. | Frame it as fixing, training or normalizing the wearer. | Milton 2012; Crompton 2020/2025; Williams & Gilbert 2020; arXiv 2601.17946 |
| 2 | Make help **pull-based and private**: the user asks "what did that mean?" and gets it discreetly. Offer after-the-fact review. | Push a constant stream of emotion labels or behavior commands during conversation. | Begel 2020 (distraction, cognitive overhead); Chile study (excessive notifications); Zolyomi 2019 |
| 3 | Give richer, contextual, uncertain interpretations with confidence levels ("could be sarcasm, or tired"). | Show 6-basic-emotion face labels as fact. These classifiers were trained on neurotypical data and have weak evidence. | Begel 2020; Transmitter on Superpower Glass; Nagy 2024; Keyes 2020 |
| 4 | Let users **write and trigger their own scripts** and goals, with feedback measured against *their* goals. | Push "correct" neurotypical behavior (smile, eye contact) or grade the user's expressiveness against neurotypical norms. | Pearson & Rose 2021; Chile study; Sasson 2017 |
| 5 | Give **simple refusal, quick off switch, reversible settings**, transparent reasoning, and no opaque automation. | Lock settings or let others configure them. | Chile study (106 adults); Jang et al. 2026 |
| 6 | Keep data with the **wearer only**; user decides on any sharing. | Build parent, employer or clinician dashboards, or log eye contact or "social performance." | Spiel 2019; Katirai 2025; Nagy 2024; Autism Glass eye-contact recording |
